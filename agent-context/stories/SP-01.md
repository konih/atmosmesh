# SP-01 — AtmosMesh Spot: a 3.3 V climate node on an ESP32-C3 SuperMini, in two variants

- **Status:** Proposed (design spec; no hardware touched)
- **Priority:** P3 — small, self-contained, and the ESP32-C3 toolchain spike R2-01 needs anyway
- **Milestone:** Spot v1
- **Depends on:** nothing reserved by Room or Room v2; photo of one SuperMini front and back
  (pin labels, OLED pins, regulator marking); one BME280 breakout photographed for its SDO/CSB pins

## User story

As the operator, I want a palm-sized station that I can build in an evening from parts nobody
else has claimed, put in any room that has a USB socket, and read on its own little screen or in
Home Assistant, so that the rooms without a Room-class station still get temperature, humidity and
pressure; and I want one of those stations to also hold a sensor out of the window on a wire, so
that the outside temperature and humidity come from the same node without any electronics
leaving the room.

## Outcome

One carrier layout on a 14 × 20 hole perfboard, built in two population variants:

| Variant | Product id | On the board | On the second header | Extra |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Spot In** | `atmosmesh-spot` | SuperMini + BME280 at `0x76` | VEML7700 or SHT41 directly plugged, or empty | optional DS18B20 probe, optional soil header |
| **Spot In/Out** | `atmosmesh-spot-out` | SuperMini + BME280 at `0x76` (inside) | second BME280 at `0x77` on a cable of up to about 3 m (outside) | optional DS18B20 probe |

Everything is one 3.3 V domain. Firmware is one product with a variant flag, built with a
PlatformIO `esp32-c3` environment, publishing on the existing MQTT contract, with the on-board
72 × 40 OLED cycling through the values. Five SuperMinis and six BME280 in stock cover, for
example, one In/Out unit and three In units with a BME280 to spare.

## Why this project and not another

| Candidate | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **BME280 climate node** (this story) | Uses the two largest unreserved piles (5 SuperMinis, 6 BME280). I²C only, 3.3 V only, three parts on the board. Repeatable. Proves the C3 toolchain |
| Node potted in epoxy and mounted outside | Rejected. Epoxy traps heat, is irreversible, cracks at the USB socket, and any coating over the ceramic antenna detunes the board's weakest part. The In/Out variant gets the outdoor reading with nothing electronic outside but a breakout in a vented shield |
| Soil node with the capacitive modules | Grove and Aqua already own soil; on the Spot it is an optional header, not the product |
| BMI160 tilt or vibration node | No question in the roadmap needs it |
| 433 MHz receiver logger (H3V4F is 3.3 V) | Fun, but a decoder project, not an AtmosMesh station |

## Controller: ESP32-C3 SuperMini OLED (type knowledge until photographed)

- 3.3 V logic and a small on-board LDO from USB 5 V. **The `5V` pin is never wired to anything on
  the carrier.**
- OLED: SSD1306 driving a 72 × 40 panel with a column offset; some libraries name the same panel
  "70 × 42". I²C `0x3C`, usually on **GPIO5 (SDA) / GPIO6 (SCL)**. One board is plugged in on the
  bench as of 4 September: its bus scan and the seller's example sketch settle the address and the
  two GPIOs, and that closes open question 1. The sensors share this bus, so the OLED's own
  pull-ups count toward the bus total.
- Strapping pins **GPIO2, GPIO8, GPIO9** stay unused as inputs. GPIO8 drives the on-board LED,
  GPIO9 is the BOOT button — the display page button in firmware, nothing on the carrier.
- ADC1 on GPIO0–4. GPIO4 is the 1-Wire pin, GPIO3 the soil ADC input, GPIO1 the soil power
  switch. GPIO20/21 are the USB-serial UART on some revisions and stay free.
- Known SuperMini trait: the ceramic antenna couples badly and Wi-Fi drops at full TX power.
  Firmware limits TX power to about 8.5 dBm at start-up as part of the product.
- Pin rows: 8 pins each, assumed **6 pitches (15.24 mm) apart** — dry-fit before anything is
  soldered, exactly as Room's perfboard plan demands for the ideaspark board.

## Sensor set and I²C map

| Device | Address | Header | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BME280, inside | `0x76` (SDO low, the breakout default) | `J_BME` 4-pin: VIN, GND, SCL, SDA | Feed **3V3**. Breakout regulator and pull-ups to photograph; most carry 10 kΩ pull-ups. Pressure is the value Room does not have |
| BME280, outside (In/Out only) | `0x77` (**SDO tied to 3V3**) | `J_OUT` 4-pin: VIN, GND, SCL, SDA, to the cable | Two BME280 collide at `0x76`; the outdoor breakout's SDO pin is strapped high. Six-pin GY-BME280 breakouts expose SDO and CSB (CSB to 3V3 for I²C); four-pin ones have a solder jumper for it. Photograph before strapping |
| OLED on the SuperMini | `0x3C` | on board | Shares the bus. A scan must show it before any sensor is fitted |
| Optional VEML7700 (In only) | `0x10` | `J_OUT` used as a plain second header | Two free after Room v2. Its `3Vo` pin stays off the header |
| Optional SHT41 (In only) | `0x44` | `J_OUT` used as a plain second header | Reference humidity beside the BME280 |
| Optional DS18B20 probe | 1-Wire, GPIO4 | `J_1W` 3-pin: 3V3, DQ, GND with `R_1W` 4.7 kΩ to 3V3 | Two probes with 2.5 m cable in stock. Surface or water temperature; cheap to leave the header on either variant |
| Optional capacitive soil module (In only) | analog, GPIO3 | `J_SOIL` 3-pin: VCC, GND, AOUT | `VCC` fed from GPIO1 through nothing but the pin (the module draws ~5 mA) so firmware can power it only around a reading. Not a corrosion measure — the operator judges the capacitive probes safe for continuous power — but it saves the current and gives a clean "sensor absent" test. Read the timer chip on the module: TL555/TLC555 work at 3.3 V, a plain NE555 does not |

No address collides in either variant. Pull-ups are populated per variant (below).

## The outdoor cable (In/Out variant)

I²C is a board-level bus, so the cable is treated as the hostile part of this design:

- **Length up to about 3 m.** Cat5 or any 4-core cable adds roughly 50 pF/m; 3 m plus the two
  breakouts stays under the 400 pF I²C limit. Longer needs a bus extender, which is not in stock.
- **Pairing.** If the cable is twisted pairs, put **SDA with GND** on one pair and **SCL with 3V3**
  on another. Never twist SDA with SCL: that is the crosstalk case.
- **Pull-ups 2.2 kΩ** on the carrier for this variant (`R_PU_SDA`/`R_PU_SCL`), on the board side
  of the series resistors. With two 10 kΩ breakout pairs in parallel the total lands near 1.6 kΩ,
  about 2 mA sink at 3.3 V, inside what the C3 pins drive comfortably.
- **Series 100 Ω** (`R_OUT_SDA`, `R_OUT_SCL`) between the bus and `J_OUT`, at the board end. They
  damp the cable and limit fault current if the cable is shorted or pinched in the window frame.
- **Bus speed 50 kHz** for this variant, 100 kHz for Spot In. The firmware sets it from the
  variant flag.
- **Optional clamps.** No Zener below 5.1 V is in stock (the inventory records that rule), so the
  clamp is the rail-to-rail kind: for each of SDA and SCL at the board end, one 1N5819 from the
  line to 3V3 (band toward 3V3) and one from GND to the line (band toward the line). With the
  100 Ω in series, a static discharge into the cable is clamped to about 0.3 V outside the rails
  before it reaches the C3. Four diodes from the assortment; fit them if the cable run is long or
  exposed, and record whether they are fitted.
- **Outdoor end.** The breakout hangs in free air inside a vented shield under the eaves or the
  window sill, out of rain and sun: a stack of plates or an inverted cup. No coating on the
  sensor; the housing is the protection. The cable enters through a gland or a drip loop so water
  runs off the cable, not into the shield.
- **Nothing else outside.** No controller, no display, no coating decisions. The window frame gets
  a flat cable, and the node stays on the sill inside.

Firmware treats `0x77` disappearing as "outdoor sensor unavailable", not as a bus fault: the cable
is the part most likely to be unplugged.

## Board layout — 14 × 20 holes (35.6 × 50.8 mm), one layout for both variants

Columns 1–14 left to right, rows 1–20 top to bottom, viewed from the component side, USB toward
row 1. The 10 × 24 board also fits (module rows at columns 2 and 8, headers in rows 11–24) but
leaves no clearance beside the antenna end, so 14 × 20 is the recommendation.

| Ref | Spot In | Spot In/Out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `R_PU_SDA`, `R_PU_SCL` | 4.7 kΩ, or unpopulated if the boards' own pull-ups already sum to ~5 kΩ | 2.2 kΩ |

| `J_SOIL`, GPIO1/GPIO3 | optional | not fitted |
| `J_1W`, `R_1W` | optional | optional |

## Power

USB only. Every sensor draws about a milliampere, the OLED a few, the soil module ~5 mA while
powered. The only real load is the C3's Wi-Fi transmit burst (up to ~350 mA) on the SuperMini's
small LDO, which is why the TX-power limit matters for supply as well as for range. No bulk
capacitor beyond `C1` is planned; if the board browns out on connect, add 10 µF at `TP_3V3` and
record it. The outdoor BME280 adds nothing measurable to the budget.

## Firmware

- New composition root `firmware/src/products/atmosmesh_spot.cpp` per ADR-0001, one build flag
  for the variant, new PlatformIO environment for the C3 (USB CDC on boot). **The first
  acceptance criterion is a clean build and a serial hello on real hardware**: that is the
  toolchain spike.
- Reuse the I²C scan-and-report behaviour and the MQTT contract shape (`{value, unit, valid,
  age_ms}`, `id`, `device`). Station ids from `atmosmesh-0004` up, one per unit.
- Entities, Spot In: `temperature`, `humidity`, `pressure` (hPa, with the altitude-corrected
  `pressure_sea_level` as a second entity); `lux` or `temperature_sht41`/`humidity_sht41` if the
  second header is populated; `temperature_probe`; `soil_raw` (raw ADC, not a moisture claim,
  the Grove rule).
- Entities, Spot In/Out: `temperature_in`, `humidity_in`, `pressure`, `temperature_out`,
  `humidity_out`, `temperature_probe`. Outdoor values go `valid: false` the moment `0x77` stops
  answering, and the node keeps publishing the indoor ones.
- OLED: pages rotated every few seconds, the BOOT button forces the next one. In/Out shows
  "in" and "out" on separate pages in a large font; 72 × 40 holds one value legibly, not four.
- Bus speed from the variant flag: 100 kHz In, 50 kHz In/Out.
- Wi-Fi TX power limited at start-up; RSSI logged with every publish.

## In scope

- One carrier layout; the first build is a **Spot In** on the bench next to Room for the
  comparison criterion, the second an **In/Out** on a window sill.
- Photo identification of the SuperMini and of one BME280 breakout (pins, pull-ups, regulator,
  SDO/CSB), and the outdoor breakout strapped to `0x77` and re-scanned before it goes on the cable.
- PlatformIO C3 environment, host-tested BME280 compensation math (the Bosch integer formulas are
  a classic source of off-by-a-factor bugs), MQTT discovery for the new product.
- Cable: 3 m or less, pairing as above, drip loop, vented shield for the outdoor breakout.

## Out of scope

- Battery operation. The SuperMini has no charger, and deep-sleep with an OLED is a different
  product.
- Any electronics outside besides the breakout. Coating or potting the node is rejected above.
- The ESP32-C6 modules. Same spike, different chip; after the C3 works.
- Any 5 V sensor. The board has no 5 V domain by design.
- Cable runs beyond about 3 m, which need a bus extender that is not in stock.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Given the PlatformIO C3 environment, when the product builds and flashes, then the serial
      log shows the product name, station id, variant and a bus scan on real hardware.
- [ ] Given the SuperMini photographed, when the OLED pins and pull-ups are recorded in
      `docs/hardware/inventory.md`, then the carrier's `R_PU_*` value per variant is written down.
- [ ] Given the bus scanned with only the OLED fitted, then exactly `0x3C` answers; with the
      inside BME280 fitted, `0x76` joins it; on In/Out with the cable connected, `0x77` joins too.
- [ ] Given the In/Out cable at its final length, when SDA is captured on the DSO2D15 at 50 kHz,
      then the rise time is under 1 µs and the low level under 0.4 V at the board end.
- [ ] Given the node beside Room's SHT41 for one hour, when the readings are compared, then
      temperature agrees within 1 °C and humidity within 5 %RH, or the difference is explained.
- [ ] Given pressure published for 24 hours, then `pressure_sea_level` tracks the local weather
      service within a few hPa.
- [ ] Given the outdoor breakout in its shield for 24 hours including a night, then
      `temperature_out` follows the weather service's trend and `humidity_out` returns from its
      night-time maximum by midday, or the shield placement is changed and the test repeated.
- [ ] Given Wi-Fi at limited TX power, when the node runs for 24 hours, then no reconnect gap
      longer than 5 minutes appears in the log.
- [ ] Given the outdoor cable unplugged at runtime, then the indoor entities keep updating and the
      outdoor ones report unavailable within one publish interval.

## Validation

- Automated: BME280 compensation against the datasheet's worked example; MQTT payload schema;
  unavailable-device behaviour for each sensor independently; variant flag selects bus speed and
  entity set.
- Manual: bus scans per stage, cable rise-time capture, side-by-side comparison with Room,
  24-hour pressure and outdoor traces, RSSI log.
- Failure/edge case: any breakout unplugged at runtime; USB power cycled (reconnects without
  manual reset); outdoor cable shorted SDA to GND (the 100 Ω resistor limits it and the bus
  recovers when the short clears — verify on the bench, not by accident).
