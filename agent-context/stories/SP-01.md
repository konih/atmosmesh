# SP-01 — AtmosMesh Spot: a 3.3 V climate node on an ESP32-C3 SuperMini

- **Status:** Proposed (design spec; no hardware touched)
- **Priority:** P3 — small, self-contained, and the ESP32-C3 toolchain spike R2-01 needs anyway
- **Milestone:** Spot v1
- **Depends on:** nothing reserved by Room or Room v2; photo of one SuperMini front and back
  (pin labels, OLED pins, regulator marking)

## User story

As the operator, I want a palm-sized station that I can build in an evening from parts nobody
else has claimed, put in any room that has a USB socket, and read on its own little screen or in
Home Assistant, so that the rooms without a Room-class station still get temperature, humidity and
pressure — and so that the ESP32-C3 build chain is proven on something small before Room v2 needs
a decision about C3 or C6 boards.

## Outcome

A 14 × 20 hole perfboard carrying one ESP32-C3 SuperMini OLED board, one BME280 on a 4-pin
header, one spare 4-pin I²C header for a VEML7700 or SHT41, and nothing that is not 3.3 V.
Firmware product `atmosmesh-spot` built with a PlatformIO `esp32-c3` environment, publishing on
the existing MQTT contract, with the on-board 72 × 40 OLED cycling through the values. Five
SuperMinis and six BME280 in stock mean the design can be repeated for five rooms without buying
anything.

## Why this project and not another

| Candidate | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **BME280 climate node** (this story) | Uses the two largest unreserved piles (5 SuperMinis, 6 BME280). I²C only, 3.3 V only, three parts on the board. Repeatable. Proves the C3 toolchain |
| Soil node with the capacitive modules | Grove and Aqua already own soil; a third soil product adds nothing to the evidence base |
| BMI160 tilt or vibration node | No question in the roadmap needs it |
| 433 MHz receiver logger (H3V4F is 3.3 V) | Fun, but a decoder project, not an AtmosMesh station |
| SGP40/SGP41 VOC node | Both are trend sensors; the ENS160 already covers that role in Room v2, and one ENS160+AHT20 stays free for a Spot variant later |

The Spot's second header is what keeps those options open: any 3.3 V I²C breakout can be plugged
in later without touching the board.

## Controller: ESP32-C3 SuperMini OLED (type knowledge until photographed)

- 3.3 V logic and a small on-board LDO from USB 5 V. **The `5V` pin is never wired to anything on
  the carrier.** The whole board is one 3.3 V domain.
- OLED: 72 × 40, SSD1306-class, I²C `0x3C`, usually on **GPIO5 (SDA) / GPIO6 (SCL)**. Read the
  seller pinout and the silkscreen; revisions differ. The sensors share that bus, so the OLED's
  own pull-ups (if fitted) count toward the bus total.
- Strapping pins **GPIO2, GPIO8, GPIO9** stay unused as inputs. GPIO8 drives the on-board LED,
  GPIO9 is the BOOT button — usable as the display page button in firmware, not on the carrier.
- ADC1 on GPIO0–4; GPIO20/21 are the USB-serial UART on some revisions. Neither is used here.
- Known SuperMini trait: the ceramic antenna couples badly and Wi-Fi drops at full TX power.
  Firmware limits TX power to about 8.5 dBm at start-up; treat this as part of the product, not
  a tweak.
- Pin rows: 8 pins each, assumed **6 pitches (15.24 mm) apart** — dry-fit before anything is
  soldered, exactly as Room's perfboard plan demands for the ideaspark board.

## Sensor set and I²C map

| Device | Address | Header | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BME280 | `0x76` (SDO low) or `0x77` | `J_BME` 4-pin: VIN, GND, SCL, SDA | Six in stock, none reserved. Feed **3V3**. Breakout regulator and pull-ups to photograph; most carry 10 kΩ pull-ups. Pressure is the value Room does not have |
| OLED on the SuperMini | `0x3C` | on board | Shares the bus. A scan must show it before any sensor is fitted |
| Optional VEML7700 | `0x10` | `J_EXT` 4-pin: VIN, GND, SCL, SDA | Two free after Room v2. Same 5-pin module as Room; its `3Vo` pin stays off the header |
| Optional SHT41 | `0x44` | `J_EXT` | Reference humidity beside the BME280, if the room is one where humidity matters |
| Optional DS18B20 probe | 1-Wire on GPIO4 | `J_1W` 3-pin: 3V3, DQ, GND with `R_1W` 4.7 kΩ to 3V3 | Two probes with 2.5 m cable in stock: a window or radiator temperature from the same node. Fit only if wanted; the header is cheap |

No address collides. Pull-ups: the carrier fits `R_PU_SDA`/`R_PU_SCL` **4.7 kΩ** footprints but
they are populated only if the photographed boards show fewer than about two 10 kΩ pairs in total.
The bus is short (under 5 cm on the board, plus the breakout), so 100 kHz will be far inside the
rise-time limit either way.

## Board layout — 14 × 20 holes (35.6 × 50.8 mm)

Columns 1–14 left to right, rows 1–20 top to bottom, viewed from the component side, USB toward
row 1. The 10 × 24 board also fits (module rows at columns 2 and 8, headers in rows 11–24) but
leaves no clearance beside the antenna end, so 14 × 20 is the recommendation.

```text
        col 1                              col 14
       ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
row 1  │            [ USB-C edge ]            │
row 2  │       ●  U1 SuperMini  ●             │  U1 pins: rows 2–9, columns 4 and 10
  :    │       ●  8 pins/row    ●             │  (6 pitches apart — dry-fit first)
  :    │       ●  antenna end   ●             │  keep columns 1–3 and 11–14 free of
  :    │       ●  toward row 9  ●             │  copper and wire beside the antenna
row 9  │       ●                ●             │
row 10 │                                      │
row 11 │  R_PU_SDA  R_PU_SCL   (only if needed)│
row 12 │  C1 100n   TP_3V3  TP_GND            │
row 13 │                                      │
row 14 │  J_BME  ●●●●         J_EXT  ●●●●     │  VIN GND SCL SDA        VIN GND SCL SDA
row 15 │  C_BME 100n          C_EXT 100n      │
row 16 │                                      │
row 17 │  J_1W ●●●  R_1W 4k7  (optional)      │  3V3 DQ GND
row 18 │                                      │
row 19 │  label: station id, board rev        │
row 20 └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Wiring by pin name, all on one 3.3 V rail:

```text
U1 3V3 ──┬── J_BME VIN ──┤C_BME 100n── GND
         ├── J_EXT VIN ──┤C_EXT 100n── GND
         ├── J_1W  3V3 ── R_1W 4k7 ── J_1W DQ ── U1 GPIO4
         ├── C1 100n ── GND
         └── R_PU_SDA 4k7 ── SDA      R_PU_SCL 4k7 ── SCL      (populate only if needed)

U1 GPIO5 (SDA) ── J_BME SDA ── J_EXT SDA
U1 GPIO6 (SCL) ── J_BME SCL ── J_EXT SCL
U1 GND ── ground rail ── every GND
U1 5V  ── nothing
```

No series resistors on this bus: it is a few centimetres long, there is no 5 V anywhere on the
board, and the C3's GPIO absolute ratings are the only thing to protect — a wiring mistake at
3.3 V cannot exceed them.

## Power

USB only. The BME280 draws under 1 mA, the VEML7700 and SHT41 the same, the OLED a few mA. The
only real load is the C3's Wi-Fi transmit burst (up to ~350 mA) on the SuperMini's small LDO,
which is why the TX-power limit above matters for supply as well as for range. No bulk capacitor
beyond `C1` is needed; if the board browns out on connect, add 10 µF at `TP_3V3` and record it.

## Firmware

- New composition root `firmware/src/products/atmosmesh_spot.cpp` per ADR-0001, new PlatformIO
  environment for the C3 (`board = esp32-c3-devkitm-1` or the SuperMini definition, USB CDC on
  boot). **The first acceptance criterion is a clean build and a serial hello on real hardware**:
  that is the toolchain spike.
- Reuse the I²C scan-and-report behaviour and the MQTT contract shape (`{value, unit, valid,
  age_ms}`, `id`, `device`). Product `atmosmesh-spot`, station ids from `atmosmesh-0004` up, one
  per unit.
- Entities: `temperature`, `humidity`, `pressure` (hPa) from the BME280; `lux` if a VEML7700 is
  present; `temperature_sht41`/`humidity_sht41` if an SHT41 is present; `temperature_probe` if a
  DS18B20 answers. Missing devices are reported unavailable, never numeric zero.
- OLED: three pages (T/RH, pressure, network) rotated every few seconds; the BOOT button forces
  the next page. 72 × 40 is small — one value per page in a large font beats four in a small one.
- Wi-Fi TX power limited at start-up; log the RSSI every publish so the antenna weakness is
  visible in Home Assistant rather than a mystery.

## In scope

- One carrier on the 14 × 20 board; a second only after the first is in a room for a week.
- Photo identification of the SuperMini and the BME280 breakout (pins, pull-ups, regulator).
- PlatformIO C3 environment, host-tested BME280 compensation math (the Bosch integer formulas are
  a classic source of off-by-a-factor bugs), MQTT discovery for the new product.

## Out of scope

- Battery operation. The SuperMini has no charger, and deep-sleep with an OLED is a different
  product.
- The ESP32-C6 modules. Same spike, different chip; do it after the C3 works.
- Any 5 V sensor. The board has no 5 V domain by design.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Given the PlatformIO C3 environment, when the product builds and flashes, then the serial
      log shows the product name, station id and a bus scan on real hardware.
- [ ] Given the SuperMini photographed, when the OLED pins and pull-ups are recorded in
      `docs/hardware/inventory.md`, then the carrier's `R_PU_*` population decision is written down.
- [ ] Given the bus scanned with only the OLED fitted, then exactly `0x3C` answers; with the
      BME280 fitted, `0x76` or `0x77` joins it.
- [ ] Given the node beside Room's SHT41 for one hour, when the readings are compared, then
      temperature agrees within 1 °C and humidity within 5 %RH, or the difference is explained.
- [ ] Given pressure published for 24 hours, then it tracks the local weather service's
      sea-level-corrected value within a few hPa after the altitude correction is applied.
- [ ] Given Wi-Fi at limited TX power, when the node runs for 24 hours, then no reconnect gap
      longer than 5 minutes appears in the log.

## Validation

- Automated: BME280 compensation against the datasheet's worked example; MQTT payload schema;
  unavailable-device behaviour.
- Manual: bus scan, side-by-side comparison with Room, 24-hour pressure trace, RSSI log.
- Failure/edge case: BME280 unplugged at runtime (node keeps publishing the OLED page and reports
  the sensor unavailable); USB power cycled (reconnects without manual reset).
