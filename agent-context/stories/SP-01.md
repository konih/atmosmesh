# SP-01 — AtmosMesh Spot: a 3.3 V presence-and-climate node on an ESP32-C3 SuperMini

- **Status:** Proposed (design spec; no hardware touched)
- **Priority:** P3 — small, self-contained, and the ESP32-C3 toolchain spike R2-01 needs anyway
- **Milestone:** Spot v1
- **Depends on:** photo of one SuperMini front and back (pin labels, OLED pins, regulator
  marking); the LD2410S datasheet (supply, current, `OUT` level, UART); a BME280 breakout
  photographed for the In/Out cable

## User story

As the operator, I want a palm-sized station that I can build in an evening, put in any room that
has a USB socket, and read on its own little screen or in Home Assistant, so that the rooms without
a Room-class station get temperature, humidity, light and — the thing PIR never gave me — whether
somebody is actually in the room; and I want one of those stations to also hold a sensor out of
the window on a wire, so that the outside temperature and humidity come from the same node without
any electronics leaving the room.

## Outcome

One carrier layout on a 14 × 20 hole perfboard, built in two population variants:

| Variant | Product id | On the board | Second I²C header | Extra |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Spot In** | `atmosmesh-spot` | SuperMini, SHT41 at `0x44`, VEML7700 at `0x10`, HLK-LD2410S radar | empty, or any 3.3 V I²C breakout | optional DS18B20 probe |
| **Spot In/Out** | `atmosmesh-spot-out` | the same | BME280 at `0x76` on a cable of up to about 3 m, outside | optional DS18B20 probe |

Everything is one 3.3 V domain. Firmware is one product with a variant flag, built with a
PlatformIO `esp32-c3` environment, publishing on the existing MQTT contract, with the on-board
72 × 40 OLED cycling through the values.

**The first unit takes the last SHT41 spare and the only LD2410S.** After Room, Room v2 and
this unit, one SHT41 and one VEML7700 remain; further Spot units put a BME280 breakout on the
`J_SHT` header instead (same 4-pin order on the common breakouts — verify) and leave the radar
header empty or wait for a second LD2410S. That is the reservation recorded in
`docs/hardware/inventory.md`.

## Sensor choices, and why

- **SHT41, not BME280, for the room.** The operator's call: an SHT41 beside a BME280 is
  redundant, the SHT41 is the accurate one (about ±1.8 %RH, ±0.2 °C), and the pressure a BME280
  would add is the same inside as outside — so it comes from the outdoor sensor on the In/Out
  variant instead, where the BME280 is the natural choice.
- **VEML7700** for lux, as on Room. Mounted on its header facing the room, on the side away from
  the OLED so the display's own glow does not become the reading.
- **HLK-LD2410S** for presence. It is the 3.3 V, low-power member of the LD24xx family with an
  `OUT` pin and a UART, which is exactly what a board without a 5 V domain needs. There is one,
  and it was reserved for Room v2; Room v2 has a 5 V domain and takes the LD2450 instead (R2-01
  updated). Its supply current, `OUT` level and UART framing come from the datasheet, which is
  the first dependency above.
- **Not potted, not outside.** Coating the ceramic antenna detunes the SuperMini's weakest part;
  the In/Out variant gets the outdoor reading with nothing electronic outside but a breakout in a
  vented shield.
- **Soil header dropped.** Three sensors and a radar fill the small board; a plant node is a
  different population and can be a later story.

## Controller: ESP32-C3 SuperMini OLED (type knowledge until photographed)

- 3.3 V logic and a small on-board LDO from USB 5 V. **The `5V` pin is never wired to anything on
  the carrier.**
- OLED: SSD1306 driving a 72 × 40 panel with a column offset; some libraries name the same panel
  "70 × 42". I²C `0x3C`, usually on **GPIO5 (SDA) / GPIO6 (SCL)**. One board is plugged in on the
  bench as of 4 September: its bus scan and the seller's example sketch settle the address and the
  two GPIOs, and that closes open question 1. The sensors share this bus, so the OLED's own
  pull-ups count toward the bus total.
- Strapping pins **GPIO2, GPIO8, GPIO9** stay unused as inputs. GPIO8 drives the on-board LED,
  GPIO9 is the BOOT button — the display page button in firmware, nothing on the carrier.
- GPIO assignment: GPIO5/6 I²C; **GPIO3** radar `OUT`; **GPIO1** radar `TX` → C3 UART1 RX;
  **GPIO0** C3 UART1 TX → radar `RX`; **GPIO4** 1-Wire. GPIO20/21 are UART0 and carry the boot
  log on some revisions, which is why the radar is not put there: a radar listening to the
  bootloader at 115200 baud is a way to get a mystery.
- Known SuperMini trait: the ceramic antenna couples badly and Wi-Fi drops at full TX power.
  Firmware limits TX power to about 8.5 dBm at start-up as part of the product.
- Pin rows: 8 pins each, assumed **6 pitches (15.24 mm) apart** — dry-fit before anything is
  soldered, exactly as Room's perfboard plan demands for the ideaspark board.

## Sensor set, bus map and headers

| Device | Address / pin | Header | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SHT41 | `0x44` | `J_SHT` 4-pin: VIN, GND, SCL, SDA | Room's operator-confirmed order; re-check on the spare. Accepts a 4-pin BME280 breakout on later units if the order matches |
| VEML7700 | `0x10` | `J_VEML` 5-pin: VIN, 3Vo (NC), GND, SCL, SDA | Room's confirmed order; `3Vo` never wired |
| HLK-LD2410S | `OUT` → GPIO3, UART → GPIO1/GPIO0 | `J_RAD` 5-pin: VCC, GND, OUT, RX, TX (**wire by label**, names vary) | 3.3 V supply per the operator and the family's description — confirm from the datasheet before it is fed. `OUT` is a 3.3 V logic level, direct to GPIO3, no transistor stage needed on a 3.3 V board. `C_RAD_BULK` 100 µF at the header for its transmit bursts |
| OLED on the SuperMini | `0x3C` | on board | Shares the bus. A scan must show it before any sensor is fitted |
| BME280, outside (In/Out only) | `0x76` (SDO low, breakout default) | `J_OUT` 4-pin: VIN, GND, SCL, SDA, to the cable | No address collision with the SHT41, so no strapping. Gives `temperature_out`, `humidity_out` and `pressure` |
| Optional DS18B20 probe | 1-Wire, GPIO4 | `J_1W` 3-pin: 3V3, DQ, GND with `R_1W` 4.7 kΩ to 3V3 | Surface or water temperature on a 2.5 m cable |

No address collides in either variant. Pull-ups are populated per variant (below).

Radar placement rules, from Room v2's lesson in reverse: the antenna face looks into the room
with no metal or copper in front of it, the module stands on its header at the board edge or on a
short lead, and it must not look across a fan, a curtain in a draught or a pet's bed — a 24 GHz
radar reports every one of those as a moving target. Presence is confirmed with a person sitting
still and with the room empty before it drives anything.

## The outdoor cable (In/Out variant)

I²C is a board-level bus, so the cable is treated as the hostile part of this design:

- **Length up to about 3 m.** Cat5 or any 4-core cable adds roughly 50 pF/m; 3 m plus the
  breakouts stays under the 400 pF I²C limit. Longer needs a bus extender, which is not in stock.
- **Pairing.** If the cable is twisted pairs, put **SDA with GND** on one pair and **SCL with 3V3**
  on another. Never twist SDA with SCL: that is the crosstalk case.
- **Pull-ups 2.2 kΩ** on the carrier for this variant (`R_PU_SDA`/`R_PU_SCL`), on the board side
  of the series resistors. With the breakout pull-ups in parallel the total lands near 1.6 kΩ,
  about 2 mA sink at 3.3 V, inside what the C3 pins drive comfortably.
- **Series 100 Ω** (`R_OUT_SDA`, `R_OUT_SCL`) between the bus and `J_OUT`, at the board end. They
  damp the cable and limit fault current if the cable is shorted or pinched in the window frame.
- **Bus speed 50 kHz** for this variant, 100 kHz for Spot In. The firmware sets it from the
  variant flag.
- **Optional clamps.** No Zener below 5.1 V is in stock (the inventory records that rule), so the
  clamp is the rail-to-rail kind: for each of SDA and SCL at the board end, one 1N5819 from the
  line to 3V3 (band toward 3V3) and one from GND to the line (band toward the line). With the
  100 Ω in series, a static discharge into the cable is clamped to about 0.3 V outside the rails
  before it reaches the C3. Four diodes from the assortment; fit them if the cable run is long or
  exposed, and record whether they are fitted.
- **Outdoor end.** The breakout hangs in free air inside a vented shield under the eaves or the
  window sill, out of rain and sun: a stack of plates or an inverted cup. No coating on the
  sensor; the housing is the protection. The cable enters through a gland or a drip loop so water
  runs off the cable, not into the shield.
- **Nothing else outside.** No controller, no display, no coating decisions. The window frame gets
  a flat cable, and the node stays on the sill inside.

Firmware treats `0x76` disappearing as "outdoor sensor unavailable", not as a bus fault: the cable
is the part most likely to be unplugged.

## Board layout — 14 × 20 holes (35.6 × 50.8 mm), one layout for both variants

Columns 1–14 left to right, rows 1–20 top to bottom, viewed from the component side, USB toward
row 1. The 10 × 24 board also fits (module rows at columns 2 and 8, headers in rows 11–24) but
leaves no clearance beside the antenna end, so 14 × 20 is the recommendation.

`J_VEML` and `J_RAD` sit on opposite edges of the lower half so the light sensor can face one way
and the radar the other; on a windowsill unit the radar faces the room and the VEML7700 faces up.

## Power

USB only. The SHT41 and VEML7700 draw about a milliampere each, the OLED a few, the outdoor
BME280 under one. The radar is the unknown: the LD2410S is the family's low-power member, but its
transmit bursts are why `C_RAD_BULK` sits at its header and why its datasheet current is a
dependency. The other real load is the C3's Wi-Fi transmit burst (up to ~350 mA) on the
SuperMini's small LDO, which is why the TX-power limit matters for supply as well as for range.
If the board browns out on connect, add 10 µF at `TP_3V3` and record it.

## Firmware

- New composition root `firmware/src/products/atmosmesh_spot.cpp` per ADR-0001, one build flag
  for the variant, new PlatformIO environment for the C3 (USB CDC on boot). **The first
  acceptance criterion is a clean build and a serial hello on real hardware**: that is the
  toolchain spike.
- Reuse the I²C scan-and-report behaviour and the MQTT contract shape (`{value, unit, valid,
  age_ms}`, `id`, `device`). Station ids from `atmosmesh-0004` up, one per unit.
- Entities, Spot In: `temperature`, `humidity` (SHT41), `lux`, `presence` (radar `OUT`, with a
  hold time), `temperature_probe` if a DS18B20 answers. Optional `distance` and `energy` from the
  LD2410S UART once its frames are decoded — the product works on `OUT` alone first, the plan
  Room v2 had to give up when it lost this radar.
- Entities, Spot In/Out: the above plus `temperature_out`, `humidity_out`, `pressure` and the
  altitude-corrected `pressure_sea_level`. Outdoor values go `valid: false` the moment `0x76`
  stops answering, and the node keeps publishing the indoor ones.
- OLED: pages rotated every few seconds, the BOOT button forces the next one; a presence glyph
  in the corner of every page. 72 × 40 holds one value legibly, not four.
- Bus speed from the variant flag: 100 kHz In, 50 kHz In/Out.
- Wi-Fi TX power limited at start-up; RSSI logged with every publish.

## In scope

- One carrier layout; the first build is a **Spot In** on the bench next to Room for the
  comparison criteria, the second an **In/Out** on a window sill.
- Photo identification of the SuperMini and of one BME280 breakout; the LD2410S datasheet read
  and filed under `docs/hardware/datasheets/`.
- PlatformIO C3 environment, host-tested Sensirion CRC and BME280 compensation math, MQTT
  discovery for the new product.
- Cable: 3 m or less, pairing as above, drip loop, vented shield for the outdoor breakout.

## Out of scope

- Battery operation. The SuperMini has no charger, and deep-sleep with an OLED and a radar is a
  different product.
- Any electronics outside besides the breakout. Coating or potting the node is rejected above.
- The ESP32-C6 modules. Same spike, different chip; after the C3 works.
- Any 5 V sensor, including the LD2450. The board has no 5 V domain by design.
- Cable runs beyond about 3 m, which need a bus extender that is not in stock.
- Radar zones, distance thresholds and multi-target anything.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Given the PlatformIO C3 environment, when the product builds and flashes, then the serial
      log shows the product name, station id, variant and a bus scan on real hardware.
- [ ] Given the SuperMini photographed, when the OLED pins and pull-ups are recorded in
      `docs/hardware/inventory.md`, then the carrier's `R_PU_*` value per variant is written down.
- [ ] Given the LD2410S datasheet, when supply voltage, current and `OUT` level are recorded in
      `docs/hardware/inventory.md`, then the radar header is either approved for 3.3 V or the
      radar is struck from this story.
- [ ] Given the bus scanned with only the OLED fitted, then exactly `0x3C` answers; with the
      sensors fitted, `0x44` and `0x10` join it; on In/Out with the cable connected, `0x76` too.
- [ ] Given the In/Out cable at its final length, when SDA is captured on the DSO2D15 at 50 kHz,
      then the rise time is under 1 µs and the low level under 0.4 V at the board end.
- [ ] Given the node beside Room's SHT41 for one hour, when the readings are compared, then
      temperature agrees within 0.5 °C and humidity within 3 %RH, or the difference is explained.
- [ ] Given a person sitting still for 5 minutes, then `presence` stays `on`; given the room
      empty for 5 minutes with any fan or curtain in the radar's view noted, it is `off`.
- [ ] Given the outdoor breakout in its shield for 24 hours including a night, then
      `temperature_out` follows the weather service's trend and `humidity_out` returns from its
      night-time maximum by midday, or the shield placement is changed and the test repeated.
- [ ] Given Wi-Fi at limited TX power, when the node runs for 24 hours, then no reconnect gap
      longer than 5 minutes appears in the log.
- [ ] Given the outdoor cable unplugged at runtime, then the indoor entities keep updating and the
      outdoor ones report unavailable within one publish interval.

## Validation

- Automated: Sensirion CRC and BME280 compensation against datasheet examples; MQTT payload
  schema; unavailable-device behaviour for each sensor independently; variant flag selects bus
  speed and entity set; presence hold-time logic.
- Manual: bus scans per stage, radar `OUT` idle and active levels with the meter before GPIO3 is
  connected, cable rise-time capture, side-by-side comparison with Room, 24-hour outdoor trace,
  presence still-sitting and empty-room tests, RSSI log.
- Failure/edge case: any breakout unplugged at runtime; USB power cycled (reconnects without
  manual reset); outdoor cable shorted SDA to GND (the 100 Ω resistor limits it and the bus
  recovers when the short clears — verify on the bench, not by accident); radar `OUT` stuck high.
